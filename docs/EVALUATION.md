## Observations

Comparisons computed from the same result files.

- E2 `clean`: the naive baseline has higher recall than the matcher (0.986 against 0.980) at precision 0.999958 against 1.000000. On this synthetic world the matcher's abstention lowers recall without a material gain in precision.
- E2 `faults_5pct`: the naive baseline has higher recall than the matcher (0.722 against 0.676) at precision 0.999933 against 1.000000. On this synthetic world the matcher's abstention lowers recall without a material gain in precision.
- E3: the highest recall of planted blackspots with exposure comes from `rate` (0.520). Empirical Bayes ranks by excess crashes, so a planted blackspot on a quiet or short segment, whose excess is small in absolute terms, can rank below busy segments with a modest excess.

## Limitations

- All results hold for the synthetic world in this repository only. The world, the faults, the rules, the adapters and the ranking method were built by the same author, so agreement between them is expected to be better than on real feeds.
- Fault detection (E1) measures the faults in the catalogue. It says nothing about defects that were not anticipated.
- Entity resolution (E2) uses matching parameters that are assumptions, not fitted values; real producers may have larger time and location differences.
- The synthetic crash process has the same functional form as the safety performance function, which favours Empirical Bayes in E3. On real data the SPF would be misspecified to some degree.
- Daily traffic is constant across 2021 to 2025 in the synthetic world and is estimated from one week of counts; real exposure varies by season and year.
- Capture recapture assumes independent detection by producers; in the synthetic world detection is independent by construction, which real reporting is not.
